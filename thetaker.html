The Taker

A short story I wrote for my college's writers club contest. The theme was metamorphosis and I actually ended up winning! Content Warnings: Strong Language, Graphic Violence, Gore, Body Horror, Death. Please read at your own discretion.

There is a moment in life when all that matters are the things you didn't do.

Some are important.

I didn't learn to drive a car.

I didn't go to college and get a degree.

I didn't tell my mom, "I love her," one last time.

And others are stupid.

I didn't get asked to prom.

I didn't get the new iPhone I wanted for Christmas.

I didn't have a first kiss.

But the same two words come before each desperate attempt to reason with the universe.

I didn't. I didn't. I didn't.

The rusted double-A batteries lying in my dirty hands agree with me.

The crackle of lightning distracts me from my spiral. I instinctively reach for the gas mask across the tattered gas station floor. Six months ago, you would've found me browsing the aisles, maybe with a friend or two, grabbing snacks for a late-afternoon study session. Now, the dilapidated building is a cruel echo of what it once was.

I yank the mask around my patchy head, snapping the elastic into place. It isn’t much protection, but it’s all I have.

I cradle my knees close to myself, leaning back on one of the destroyed store aisles. The burns hidden under my clothes ache as I move, and I take a few deep breaths through the mask. My only company are the cereal boxes strewn across the floor.

Their silent presence reminds me of the bodies that littered the streets right after the bombs fell. Burned and gutted, the stench somehow stronger than their horrific presence.

I guess cornflakes are blood in this disgusting analogy I created.

They — the dead people, I mean — were lucky. So fucking lucky.

They left before the Takers showed up.

They grab you, begging for water, or food, or drugs, or anything worth meaning in this world. They wander the surface, naked and scorched, eyes glazed, and moral reasoning long gone. All they have is the innate human desire to stay alive. And they will take from you until all that is left is the blood on the ground.

Some survivors called them "ferals," but I preferred my name for them.

They claimed a boy earlier today — interrupting my attempt at filtering acid rain through a coffee filter. Not my best moment, but I had gone three days without water.

When the Takers find someone, you always hear it first.

The panicked screams.

The crunch of someone's throat when a foot stomps on it.

The choke of blood in someone's mouth as they beg you to stop ripping their fingers off.

The tearing noise that cartilage makes as ears, noses, or anything soft is torn away.

The Takers barely left him with anything by the time I had gotten to him.

But the worst part was that I had recognized him.

His name was Luke, or maybe Brian, and he sat behind me in my English class. He never paid attention — usually just laughed at whatever shit his buddy showed him on his phone.

Here he was, six months later, at the mercy of the girl in class he had never spoken to.

One of his mangled arms reached for me, fingers bent in unnatural ways. His broken ribs rattled in his chest as he wheezed. His bloodshot eyes locked with mine. I didn't know if he had recognized me, or was just begging ‌for me to end his life faster.

We were the same in a sense. Soliciting on death's door and knocking obnoxiously.

Tears drew clean lines through the filth on his face.

“Please... Please don’t,” he pleaded, the words garbled from the fresh blood spilling from his lips.

Maybe we could have been friends in another life.

His other hand gripped something. Something that the Takers had missed. The bloody hand pulled so tight that his broken white bones poked through the skin, catching the dying sunlight.

Crouching to see what it finally was, I pulled out the EDC knife in my pocket and began to cut into his fingers.

I didn't hesitate

I didn't think twice.

I didn't apologize.

I didn't. I didn't. I didn't.

He writhed and groaned, but I had gotten it done.

Took what needed to be taken.

I stare at the blood-stained batteries in my hand as thunder booms outside the gas station.

I had become a Taker.

Just like them.
